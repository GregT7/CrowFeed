# Product Brief: CrowFeed

## Executive Summary

CrowFeed is a portable weatherproof box Greg carries between his apartment porch and the lake. It records the birds and puts out food. One dose slides onto an open tray. The bird can always step back from it.

The porch is a cave, open only forward onto a concrete ledge, and the crows that know it carry shelled peanuts up to the roof. Blue jays come onto the same balcony. The lake may have no wifi. A hopper left open, or a feeder that only works through a home app, does not fit either place. A part that can close on a beak, foot, or wing is unacceptable.

This brief is the build Greg wants for himself, because the idea is cool. Apartment mode waits for the camera to call the visitor a crow. Lake mode refills an empty dish. Lights on the outside of the box show that it is on, that it needs a refill, and that it is in lock mode.

## The Problem

Greg wants the same birds on camera and a reliable piece of food in front of them, in two places that behave differently.

On the porch, an empty ledge should get nothing. A crow at the dish should get one piece. A blue jay will sometimes get one too, and one miss is acceptable. A streak of misses empties the box, and that is the failure. Crows on the roof cannot see the porch, so another crow learns the spot by watching a bird arrive overhead with a peanut, not by watching the tray.

At the lake, the dish should fill itself whenever it is empty, and recording starts when Greg says so. Wifi is not part of that place. The physical button on the box has to be enough.

Hand feeding can do the peanut and the timing, and it does not leave a photo of the dose or a clip of the visit. Store-bought camera feeders leave the seed open for songbirds and expect a home network. Nobody sells the box this brief describes.

## The Solution

The enclosure is for weather, refill, and carrying. Food sits in a store. A covered latch meters one dose, and gravity carries it to an open spot in frame. The latch never covers the bird.

One food at a time. The first fill is shelled peanuts. The same compartment can later hold mealworms or blueberries, after it is emptied and refilled. A peanut trail on the ground is a temporary lesson Greg does by hand. It is finished when a crow lands on the tray and the ground is bare. If the birds stop coming, he lays the trail again.

**Apartment.** The camera has to see a visitor at the dish, and the dose drops only when classification says that visitor is a crow. An empty porch gets no dose. Every dose saves a photo, including mistakes. Movement starts a video clip and a 15 second countdown. Further movement resets the countdown to 15. The clip ends at zero.

**Lake.** One dose reloads whenever the dish is empty. No classification, and no dose photos. A button starts the clip. A button stops it, or it stops after 45 minutes.

Dose photos and video stay separate. Apartment food still waits for a crow.

After four non-crow doses in a row, the tray stays quiet. A later crow classification does not clear that streak. Greg resets it with the physical button, or with a phone button when wifi is connected. The phone button is a shortcut. The button on the box is the real reset.

Three separate lights on the outside of the box, so the states can be read without opening it:

- On: the box is turned on.
- Refill: it is empty and needs more food. [ASSUMPTION] This light tracks the store behind the latch, not the open dish. The dish is supposed to run empty.
- Lock: feeding is paused. [ASSUMPTION] Lock mode is the four-strike pause, cleared by the reset button.

## What Makes This Different

The difference is the behavior, and it is personal. Commercial camera feeders in 2026 are open seed hoppers for songbirds, used with an app on home wifi. CrowFeed meters one piece onto an open tray, works where there is no wifi, and stops after a streak of wrong animals.

Telling a crow from a blue jay is the part that may fail. This brief claims no accuracy number. A miss costs one peanut, then another, and on the fourth the box waits for Greg. That limit is what keeps "reliable" from meaning the jays empty it.

There is no moat here. The advantage is that Greg is building the box he wants, because a finished version of it is not for sale.

## Who This Serves

Greg, at the apartment porch and at the lake. Success for him is a crow on the tray, a recording of the visit, and a box he can trust with the birds and read from the lights.

The other regular animal on the porch is the blue jay. A jay dose is a counted miss, not a second audience.

## Success Criteria

- A crow lands on the tray when there are no peanuts on the ground.
- On the porch, one dose reaches the open tray only after the camera classifies the visitor as a crow, and that dose has a saved photo.
- Four non-crow doses in a row leave the tray quiet until Greg presses the reset.
- At the lake, an empty dish receives one new dose, and a clip runs from the button until the button or 45 minutes.
- From outside the box, the lights show on, needs refill, and lock mode.
- No moving part closes on a beak, foot, or wing, or closes while a bird is touching the food.

## Scope

**In the first build**

- One portable weatherproof box for both places.
- Open tray, one dose from a store, one food at a time. Peanuts first. Mealworms and blueberries are possible later by emptying and refilling.
- Apartment classification, dose photo, and movement video. Lake auto-reload and button video.
- Physical reset. Phone reset only as a shortcut when wifi is connected.
- Three lights: on, needs refill, lock mode.

**Left out of the first build**

- A product for anyone else.
- Camouflage, or a perch above the railing.
- Audio as a way to decide who gets food.
- A hatch that can close on a bird.
- More than one food loaded at the same time.
- Wifi as a requirement at the lake.

## Vision

If it works, this box is how these birds get a peanut at both places. The trail is only for a bird that has not learned the tray. Greg keeps the videos and a photo of every porch dose, including the mistakes. Once the porch is known, he can change the food, still one kind at a time. It stays his.
