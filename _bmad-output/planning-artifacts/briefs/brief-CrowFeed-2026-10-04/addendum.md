# Addendum: CrowFeed

Detail kept out of the brief so a later PRD can use it. Decisions below come from the brainstorm on 2026-10-04 unless marked.

## Safety

The device cannot hurt the crows. No moving part may close on a beak, foot, or wing. Refuse any hatch that can close while a bird is touching the food. Food sits in an open tray the crow can always step back from. A covered latch may only meter a dose out of the store. It must not cover the bird. Losing one peanut to the wrong animal is acceptable.

## Place

The porch is enclosed on top, bottom, left, right, and behind. The only opening is forward, with a concrete ledge about chest height. Crows rest on the roof and cannot see the porch from there. Hide the balcony unit by placement. No built-in camouflage. No perch above the railing. A raised perch was considered and cut, because roof birds learn from a crow leaving with food, not from a bird standing on the tray.

## Training

The peanut trail is temporary. Use it to get attention, then stop once crows come on their own. The lesson has stuck when a crow lands on the tray and there are no peanuts on the ground. If they stop coming after the trail ends, resume the trail instead of redesigning the box. Crows carry shelled peanuts from the porch up to the roof. Other crows learn by seeing that bird arrive on the roof with food. No other foods have been tried yet.

## Dose and food

One piece on the open tray, in frame. The rest of the fill stays back and releases one dose. Gravity carries it to the open spot. One food at a time: empty the compartment and refill to change foods. The device must be able to serve peanuts, mealworms, and blueberries. The first fill is shelled peanuts only.

## Modes

Apartment food still waits for a crow. Lake food does not. Dose photos and video stay separate.

**Apartment (porch).** Drop one dose only when classification says the visitor at the dish is a crow. An empty porch gets no dose. Worthy means object classification from the camera. No audio detection. Save a photo of every dose, including mistakes. A grackle getting one is acceptable, and grackles have not been seen near the apartment. The miss that matters is a blue jay, the other animal that mainly comes onto the balcony. Video: movement starts a clip and a 15 second countdown. Further movement resets the countdown to 15. The clip ends at zero.

**Lake.** Reload one dose whenever the dish is empty. Video: a button starts the clip. A button stops it, or it stops after 45 minutes. Lake recording does not take photos.

## Pause and reset

After four non-crow doses in a row, the tray stays quiet. A blue jay dose is one strike. A later crow classification does not clear the streak. Reset is a physical button, or a phone button when wifi is connected. The physical button is the real reset, because the lake may have no wifi. The pause is a person-says-go switch. Crows-only is a preference with a leak, not a lock.

[ASSUMPTION] The brief's "lock mode" light is this pause. Greg used the word lock. The brainstorm refused the word lock for the behavior itself.

## Lights

Three separate lights on the outside of the box:

- Turned on.
- Empty and needs a refill.
- Lock mode.

[ASSUMPTION] The refill light tracks the food store, not the open dish. The dish is expected to be empty between doses, especially at the lake.

Colors, brightness, and whether a light stays lit or blinks are undecided.

## Landscape notes

Checked October 2026 for the differentiator section. Not a product decision.

Commercial smart feeders (Bird Buddy, Birdfy, FeatherSnap, Kiwibit) are Wi-Fi backyard hoppers with app species labels, roughly $100–$360, open bins, aimed at songbirds. No finished commercial single-dose crow feeder turned up. CrowBox and hobby peanut dispensers are DIY. Trail-camera AI tags a few game categories, not crow versus jay versus grackle. People who feed crows by hand put out small portions and clear leftovers.

## Undecided

Power source. Whether classification runs on the box or on another machine. Light colors and patterns.
