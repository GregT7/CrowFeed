# CrowFeed

Portable weatherproof crow feeder and trail camera.

**Center:** The device records the birds and reliably outputs food. Video is required in addition to the photo of each dose. Reliable output is one dose sliding to an open dish.

## Do not undo

- No hatch that can close on a bird. No moving part may close on a beak, foot, or wing, or close while a bird is touching the food. Food sits in an open tray the crow can always step back from. The device cannot hurt the crows. A covered latch may only meter a dose out of the store; it must not cover the bird.
- No audio detection. A visitor is worthy only by object classification from the camera.
- No built-in camouflage. Hide the balcony unit by placement.
- No perch above the railing.

## Modes

**Apartment (porch).** Food: drop one dose only when classification says the visitor at the dish is a crow. An empty porch gets no dose. Save a photo of every dose, including mistakes. Video: movement starts a clip and a 15 second countdown; further movement resets the countdown to 15; the clip ends at zero.

**Lake.** Food: reload one dose whenever the dish is empty. Video: a button starts the clip; a button stops it, or it stops after 45 minutes. Lake recording does not take photos.

Dose photos and video stay separate. Apartment food still waits for a crow. Lake food does not.

## Pause

After four non-crow doses in a row, the tray stays quiet. A blue jay dose is one strike. A later crow classification does not clear the streak. Reset is a physical button, or a phone button when wifi is connected. The physical button is the real reset; the lake may have no wifi. The pause is a person-says-go switch, not a lock. One peanut to the wrong animal is acceptable. Crows-only is a preference with a leak, not a lock.

## Dose and food

One piece on the open tray, in frame. The rest of the fill stays back and releases one dose; gravity carries it to the open spot. One food at a time: empty the compartment and refill to change foods. Must be able to serve peanuts, mealworms, and blueberries. First fill is shelled peanuts only. Enclosure is for weather, refill, and carrying.

## Training and place

Peanut trail is temporary: use it to get attention, then stop once crows come on their own. The lesson has stuck when a crow lands on the tray and there are no peanuts on the ground. If they stop coming after the trail ends, resume the trail instead of redesigning the box. Crows carry shelled peanuts from the porch up to the roof. Other crows learn by seeing that bird arrive on the roof with food, not by seeing the porch.

The porch is enclosed on top, bottom, left, right, and behind. The only opening is forward, with a concrete ledge about chest height. Crows rest on the roof and cannot see the porch from there.
