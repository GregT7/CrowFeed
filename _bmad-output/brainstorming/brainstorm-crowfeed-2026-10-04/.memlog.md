- (decision) Stance is Creative Partner. Four techniques in order: First Principles Thinking, TRIZ Contradiction, Nature's Solutions, How Might We.
- (technique) started First Principles Thinking
- (idea by coach) A crow remembers a place that startled it and will not come back.
- (idea by user) The device cannot hurt the crows.
- (idea by coach) No moving part may close on a beak, foot, or wing. The crow can always step back from the food.
- (idea by coach) Refuse any hatch that can close while a bird is touching the food. Food sits in an open tray the crow can step back from.
- (decision by user) Refuse a closing hatch.
- (idea by coach) With no hatch, a wrong animal can take one openly offered piece. The safety rule costs a peanut.
- (decision by user) Losing one peanut to the wrong animal is acceptable.
- (idea by coach) Crows-only is a preference with a leak, not a lock. A streak of misses to squirrels is a different cost than one peanut.
- (decision by user) Turn the device off after 4 wrong animals in a row.
- (idea by coach) After 4 misses the tray goes quiet until Greg resets it. A camera calling the next visitor a crow does not clear the streak by itself.
- (decision by user) Reset the pause with a physical button, or with a phone button when wifi is connected.
- (idea by coach) The physical button is the real reset because the lake may have no wifi. The phone button is only a shortcut.
- (technique) started TRIZ Contradiction
- (direction by user) The reset does not have to be useless to a stranger.
- (idea by coach) The pause is a person-says-go switch, not a lock. Next contradiction: overlooked by people on the walkway, obvious to a crow.
- (decision by user) No camouflage in the device design. On the balcony, hide it by placement. Use a trail of peanuts to get crows' attention.
- (idea by coach) The enclosure is for weather, refill, and carrying. The last peanut sits on the open tray, in frame. The trail may be training, not a permanent feature.
- (decision by user) The peanut trail is temporary. Ideally stop using it once crows come to the device on their own.
- (idea by coach) If they stop coming after the trail ends, resume the trail instead of redesigning the box. The lesson can be repeated.
- (decision by user) The trail lesson has stuck when a crow lands on the tray and there are no peanuts on the ground.
- (idea by coach) Other crows learn by watching the first bird. The trained crow replaces the peanut trail.
- (technique) started Nature's Solutions
- (decision by user) Copy the trick where other crows follow the first bird that learns the tray.
- (idea by coach) The landing spot stays visible to the flock. Hiding the box from people must not hide a feeding crow from other crows.
- (direction by user) On the balcony, the railing blocks other crows from seeing a bird on the tray.
- (idea by coach) Raise the perch above the rail so a feeding crow shows against the sky. The box stays lower and plainer.
- (decision by user) Put a perch above the balcony railing so other crows can see a bird land.
- (idea by coach) Put the peanut on the high perch, in frame. If the food stays behind the rail, the flock only sees a bird land and disappear.
- (direction by user) The porch is enclosed on top, bottom, left, right, and behind. The only opening is forward, with a concrete ledge about chest height. Crows rest on the roof and cannot see the porch from there.
- (idea by coach) A raised perch does not teach roof crows. The signal is a crow leaving the porch with food.
- (direction by user) A crow does leave the balcony and go up to the roof with food.
- (decision by user) They carry shelled peanuts up to the roof. No other foods have been tried yet.
- (idea by coach) The first version offers only shelled peanuts. Other foods wait until crows already know the porch.
- (decision by user) The device must be able to serve peanuts, mealworms, and blueberries. The first fill is peanuts only.
- (idea by coach) One compartment you empty and refill when the food changes. All three foods do not have to be loaded together.
- (decision by user) One food at a time is enough. The compartment is emptied and refilled to change foods.
- (idea by coach) How might we offer one piece on an open tray while the rest of the fill stays back, with no closing hatch.
- (technique) started How Might We
- (idea by user) A larger store releases one dose onto the tray. A covered latch meters the dose, and gravity carries it to an open spot the crow can reach.
- (decision by user) Lake mode reloads a dose whenever the dish is empty. Apartment mode drops a dose only for something worthy.
- (decision by user) No audio detection. Worthy means object classification from the camera.
- (idea by coach) An empty porch never gets a dose, because the camera has to see the object at the dish.
- (decision by user) Apartment mode drops a dose only when object classification says the visitor is a crow.
- (idea by coach) Save a still of every visitor that received a dose, including mistakes, so a grackle can be told from a crow later.
- (decision by user) Save a photo of every dose. A grackle getting one is acceptable, and grackles have not been seen near the apartment.
- (idea by coach) The classifier's real rivals are the animals that actually visit the balcony, not a general list of similar birds.
- (direction by user) Besides crows, blue jays are what mainly come onto the balcony.
- (idea by coach) A blue jay is visually distinct from a crow: smaller, blue, crested. The miss that matters is a jay getting a dose.
- (decision by user) A blue jay getting a dose counts as one of the four strikes that pause the box.
- (insight) Apartment mode pauses after four non-crow doses in a row. A crow classification does not clear the streak. Reset is the physical button, or the phone button when wifi is up.
- (insight by coach) Affinity clusters: safety (open dish, no hatch on the bird), dosing (covered latch, one dose, one food at a time, peanuts first), training (temporary peanut trail, carry shelled peanuts to the roof), place (hide by placement, porch is a cave), modes (lake auto-reload, apartment crow classification, photo per dose, four strikes, button reset). The raised perch conflicts with the cave porch.
- (decision by user) Cut the perch above the railing. The other five clusters stand: safety, dosing, training, place, and modes.
- (insight by user) What matters is that the device records the birds and reliably outputs food.
- (insight by coach) The photo of each dose is the recording and the proof the food came out. Reliable output is one dose sliding to an open dish. Apartment mode records a crow and then outputs. Lake mode outputs whenever the dish is empty. The peanut trail and the carry to the roof are how a bird arrives to be recorded. The four-strike pause is what keeps reliable from meaning the jays empty the box.
- (decision by user) Video is required in addition to the dose photo. Porch mode starts recording when movement is detected, then counts down; further movement resets the countdown to its original value. Lake mode starts when a button is pressed and records until the button is pressed again or 45 minutes pass. Lake recording does not take photos.
- (insight by coach) The dose photo and the video are separate. Porch video follows movement. Apartment food still waits for a crow classification. Lake video is a manual clip with a 45 minute cap.
- (decision by user) Porch video countdown is 15 seconds. Movement resets it to 15. The clip ends when it reaches zero.
